# Terms and Privacy: what needs an owner decision or a lawyer

The pages at `web/terms.html` and `web/privacy.html` are drafts written from what the game really does. They are not legal advice and do not claim compliance with any law. Both show a Draft notice. **Do not launch publicly until every bracket is filled and the notice is removed by a lawyer's sign-off.**

## The owner must supply
- `[OPERATOR LEGAL NAME]`, `[ADDRESS]`, `[CONTACT EMAIL]`, `[DATE]`
- `[MINIMUM AGE]`: suggested starting point for a lawyer: 18, because of crypto payments and paid cup entries

## A lawyer must review
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Refund policy | Digital-goods and crypto refunds differ by country. EU and UK rules can give a right to withdraw unless waived properly at checkout |
| Paid-entry cups | Paying to enter for a prize of purchased Gems may count as gambling or a prize competition in some places, even with no cash-out |
| Gems as non-cash items | The "no cash value" wording, and whether selling them for crypto has financial or consumer rules |
| Liability, governing law, disputes | Left blank on purpose |
| What happens to Gems if the game closes | A promise is needed either way |
| UK and EU privacy | Legal basis, data controller details, transfers outside the UK, data-subject rights. Hashed IPs and wallet addresses can still be personal data |
| Retention periods | Not yet decided |
| Sanctions and restricted countries | The game has no country blocking |
| Tax on crypto sales | Depends on where the operator is based |
| Consent banner | Google Fonts sends visitors' IP addresses to Google, which has caused complaints in the EU. Self-hosting the fonts would avoid it |
| Mind token holder rights | The game's wording should match what the collection promised holders |

## Not yet in the product
- No acceptance step at sign-up or checkout. Many lawyers will want one, recorded, before payment.
- No self-service account deletion or data export. The policy says to contact us.
